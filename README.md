## Ultrastructural organization of the crayfish claw muscle

Efforts were made to replicate the methods of Brandt et al (1965) for the benefit of correlating crayfish claw muscle physiology labs with an understanding of muscle morphology and how samples may be prepared for microscopy.

Claw muscle was exposed from both sides by cutting adjacent regions with a new razor blade and carefully cutting away the remaining shell fragments with fine scissors. The sample was immersed in fixative (2.5% glutaraldehyde, 4% paraformaldehyde, 3mM calcium chloride, 0.15M sodium cacodylate pH 7.4). The following day, the sample was further dissected into 1-3mm cross-sections. These were left in fixative for another 2 days. Samples were rinsed with buffer, stained with 2% osmium, 1.5% potassium ferrocyanide, 3mM calcium chloride, 0.15M sodium cacodylate at room temperature in the dark for 2 hours. Samples were rinsed extensively with buffer and water and then left overnight in a 1% solution of uranyl acetate in water at 4C. Samples were washed extensively, dehydrated in ethanol, and infiltrated with Embed 812 epoxy resin over the course of 2 days. Samples were then embedded and sectioned at 0.25-0.5um for light microscopy and 50-100nm for electron microscopy using an RMC ultramicrotome with a DDK tungsten carbide knife and a Diatome Histo 6mm diamond knife. 

Sections for light microscopy were stained with azure blue and basic fuscin as in Morikawa et al (2018). Sections for electron microscopy were collected on slot grids prepared as described in Dittmayer et al (2021). Images were collected using a JEOL 1011 TEM or Zeiss CrossBeam350 SEM*.

Montages were stitched using Photoshop (Adobe) Automate>Photomerge function (translation only). Data was converted to .dzi DeepZoom format and uploaded with a Leaflet.js based front end viewer (see Datasets above).
